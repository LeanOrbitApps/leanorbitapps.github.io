How to Use a DBT Diary Card on iPhone: Tracking and PDF Guide

Published on August 21, 2026 by Lean Orbit LLC

A DBT diary card is a daily record used in Dialectical Behavior Therapy. It is a simple grid for tracking emotions, urges, target behaviors, and the skills you practiced between sessions. This guide explains what goes on a diary card and how an iPhone app can make it easier to complete and share.

Your therapist likely photocopied it from a workbook by Dr. Marsha Linehan, the same way clinicians have done for thirty years. Yet this ordinary sheet of paper is quietly extraordinary. Every week, it transforms something completely invisible—the shifting, chaotic texture of a client's inner emotional life—into structured, actionable data that can be discussed during a 50-minute clinical session.

The Paper Dilemma: Why Manual Tracking Fails Client and Clinician

While the paper diary card remains a cornerstone of Dialectical Behavior Therapy (DBT), it is not without its operational bottlenecks. For clients, carrying a physical sheet of paper throughout the week can feel intrusive and vulnerable. If a client leaves their diary card on a kitchen counter or at a desk, their private emotional struggles are exposed to roommates, family members, or colleagues.

Furthermore, manual logging suffers from retrospective bias. Under stress, our memory becomes unreliable. When clients attempt to fill out a standard Linehan format diary card right before their session starts, they often suffer from "emotional recency"—oversimplifying the week's emotional spikes or forgetting the exact skills they deployed during a crisis.

For clinicians, reviewing a crumpled, handwritten, or partially filled-out sheet of paper can eat away at valuable, limited session time. Clinicians need structured, legible trends to formulate chain analyses and assess treatment compliance effectively.
